## Version 3 title

**Environment-Induced Quantum Learning (EIQL): Self-Supervised Measurement Discovery from Redundant Quantum Records**

## Scope

The first-paper scope is frozen at **theory + simulation**. A real-hardware EIQL experiment is intentionally left as future work and is not required for the logical claims in this version.

EIQL is framed as a **quantum measurement-learning framework/problem formulation**, not a new universal machine-learning paradigm.

The central distinction is:

> Redundancy across independently accessible environmental fragments generated by the same physical event supplies the self-supervised signal for discovering an initially unknown local quantum measurement/decoder.

The representation must be physically instantiated by a POVM; the learner is not merely fitting a function to already classical multiview features.

## Manuscript spine

1. research question and classical/quantum-learning scope;
2. hardware-constrained local decoder setting and operational equivalence;
3. EIQL richness-disagreement objective;
4. exact SBS pointer-information theorem;
5. robust near-SBS theorem and Bayes certificate;
6. finite-shot guarantee for a pre-specified finite decoder class;
7. theory-matched five-qubit collision benchmark;
8. analytic independent-product null and permutation diagnostics;
9. NISQ-style noise stress test;
10. Chen et al. external-architecture benchmark with independently hidden local record bases;
11. environment-only pair-moment/spectral implementation;
12. equal-copy resource comparison against a system-assisted correlation baseline;
13. measurement-setting comparison with full Pauli tomography;
14. novelty positioning, limitations, open problems, and conclusion.

## Important qualifications

Version 3 does **not** claim:

- quantum advantage;
- a new universal learning paradigm;
- unique POVM recovery;
- universal superiority over tomography;
- a theorem for arbitrary states without a nearby SBS reference;
- a real-hardware EIQL result.

The pair-moment factorization used in the resource benchmark is treated as a task-specific implementation identity rather than a standalone novelty claim pending broader spectral/covariance literature comparison.
