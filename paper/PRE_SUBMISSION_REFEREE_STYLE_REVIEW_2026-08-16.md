## Assessment

This is the strongest version of the manuscript so far. The main advance is not simply the addition of numerical material: the manuscript now closes the loop between the abstract identifiability theory and a plausible measurement workflow. The finite-shot tests, controlled conditional-correlation violation, three-view multiclass recovery, and microscopic collision-model simulation substantially strengthen the operational case for the work.

The scientific architecture is particularly strong. The manuscript separates three distinct inverse questions:

**decoder identification → ensemble identification → semantic identification.**

This distinction is introduced formally and then governs the subsequent positive and negative results rather than serving only as terminology.

The equal-prior binary result remains the cleanest centerpiece. The connected two-fragment operator factorizes as

`Omega_ij = (1/4) Delta_i tensor Delta_j`,

so two environmental fragments reveal the local Helstrom contrast directions without requiring separate reconstruction of the conditional states. The unequal-prior counterexample is an effective complement because it establishes that the equal-prior assumption marks an actual identifiability boundary.

The multiclass section is appropriately calibrated. Two views identify the discriminant subspace rather than generically identifying the full multiclass decoder. A third view supplies a standard tensor-identifiability route, while spectrum-broadcast support structure provides a distinct structured two-view mechanism. The resulting conclusion is memorable: there is no universal witness count independent of the object being identified and the geometry of the records.

The redundancy analysis is also a major strength. Product structure suppresses response coherence and support coherence and can therefore improve inverse conditioning. The mixed-state discussion correctly prevents the stronger and generally false conclusion that redundancy must always improve tomography, since product spectra can become increasingly ill-conditioned.

## Numerical evidence

The numerical section is explicitly simulation-only and tests several distinct claims rather than presenting a generic demonstration.

The binary finite-shot experiment gives log-log slopes approximately `(-0.489, -0.519, -0.489)`, consistent with the expected `N^{-1/2}` scaling, while also probing the dependence on record contrast.

The controlled conditional-correlation test probes an actual assumption violation. The reported population relation

`sin(theta_pop) ≈ 1.66 sqrt(nu) + 0.014`, with `R^2 ≈ 0.995`,

is consistent with the square-root perturbative scale suggested by the Pinsker/Wedin argument without claiming validation of the worst-case constant.

The multiclass experiment is especially useful: the exact two-view ambiguity persists to numerical precision, while a representative well-conditioned third view yields increasing finite-shot decoder recovery. This gives a direct numerical visualization of the identifiability hierarchy.

The microscopic collision-model simulation is the most important operational addition. It begins from `|+>_S |0>_E^{tensor 5}`, produces records through sequential controlled `R_y(chi)` interactions, applies unknown local `SU(2)` rotations, traces out the system, and supplies the learner only finite-shot environmental Pauli outcomes with a 2% symmetric readout-flip model. Conditional environmental states are therefore generated by dynamics rather than inserted by hand.

The phase diagram uses the strict success criterion that **all five learned decoder axes are within 5 degrees**. It exhibits the expected dependence on both record distinguishability and shot budget. The manuscript correctly describes this as a microscopic or hardware-informed simulation rather than experimental validation.

## Remaining referee-level vulnerabilities

### 1. Multiclass finite-sample scope

The three-view theorem is an identifiability result, not a complete robust finite-sample tensor-recovery theorem. The numerical study is valuable, but the abstract, discussion, and conclusion should continue to distinguish three-view identifiability from efficient or uniformly stable finite-shot learning in arbitrary multiclass models. A new major theorem is not required at this stage.

### 2. Collision-model scope

The collision study is substantially stronger than a benchmark in which conditional states are inserted by hand, but it remains a small and deliberately structured qubit model with independent fragment interactions and a simple readout-noise model. The current restraint should be preserved. It should not be presented as device-calibrated hardware validation or evidence for arbitrary correlated noise.

### 3. Experimental positioning

The paper is fundamentally theoretical quantum information. All validation remains simulation-only; the external architecture benchmark uses a published architecture rather than raw device data. The manuscript's explicit distinction between hardware-informed stress tests and experimental validation is appropriate and should not be weakened.

### 4. Density and presentation

The manuscript is mathematically and numerically dense. The preferred narrative hierarchy is

**theorems → theorem-matched simulations → microscopic collision model → external architecture benchmark.**

If additional compression is needed, the external Chen-derived benchmark and resource comparison should be shortened before removing the finite-shot, model-mismatch, multiclass, or collision-model material.

### 5. Figure readability

The information-dense semantic/intervention figure should be checked at realistic journal display size. No scientific redesign appears necessary, but small internal labels should remain legible in a two-column PDF workflow.

## Likely referee recommendation

> **Publish after minor revision.** The manuscript identifies and develops a well-defined inverse problem downstream of Quantum Darwinism: determining how redundant environmental records can themselves reveal an initially unknown readout. The distinction between decoder, ensemble, and semantic identifiability is supported by complementary positive and negative results, while the redundancy-conditioning analysis gives the framework a quantitative physical interpretation. The finite-shot, model-mismatch, multiclass, and microscopic collision-model simulations substantially strengthen the operational relevance of the theory without overstating experimental validation. I have only local requests concerning scope and presentation.

## Submission decision

The recommended next step is a final proof, notation, and figure-size audit rather than another conceptual rewrite or another major theorem. Subject to those local checks, the manuscript presents a strong scientific case for submission to PRX Quantum.
