# PRX Quantum numerical-integration note — 2026-08-15

This note is for integrating the new hardware-informed simulation suite into the current 17-page PRX Quantum manuscript, **Identifiability of Environmental Records in Quantum Darwinism: Learning Readouts from Redundancy and Intervention**.

The final manuscript source itself is not currently synchronized as editable text in this repository, so this document records manuscript-ready wording, figure priorities, and claim boundaries without overwriting the older modular Version-3 sections.

## Why these results materially improve the paper

The current manuscript is primarily analytical. Its central hierarchy distinguishes binary decoder identification, multiclass latent/decoder identification, and system-semantic identification. The new simulations now test three specific theoretical boundaries plus one end-to-end operational pipeline:

1. finite-shot scaling of the equal-prior binary connected-operator decoder;
2. robustness when conditional independence is deliberately violated;
3. exact two-view multiclass ambiguity followed by finite-shot three-view recovery;
4. explicit six-qubit system-environment collision dynamics with hidden local bases, finite Pauli counts, and readout noise.

This is stronger evidence than adding more generic noise plots because every benchmark is tied to a theorem, counterexample, or operational assumption already present in the manuscript.

## Recommended Section X restructuring

The current Section X already contains exact multiclass ambiguity, a near-SBS spectral check, the Chen-derived architecture benchmark, and the resource comparison. A stronger order is:

### X.A Exact multiclass ambiguity and finite-shot third-view recovery

Keep the existing exact counterexample, then add the new finite-shot third-view experiment immediately after it. This turns the subsection from a negative check into a complete impossibility-to-recovery narrative.

Suggested text:

> The exact commuting counterexample is not a finite-sample pathology: the two latent decompositions reconstruct the same two-view distribution to `1.39 x 10^{-17}` in max norm while their optimal local MAP rules differ. To test the corresponding positive three-view statement, we append a representative well-conditioned third stochastic view (`kappa(C)=2.5`) and estimate the required observable moments from independent samples. A spectral three-view reconstruction recovers the latent factors up to a common permutation; permutation matching to the known simulation labels is performed only after reconstruction for evaluation. Exact decoder recovery rises from `0.20` at `10^3` samples to `0.925` at `3 x 10^4` and `1.00` at `10^5`, while spectral failures fall to zero by `3 x 10^4`. The result illustrates statistical recovery in one well-conditioned generic instance; it is not a universal finite-sample efficiency claim for tensor decomposition.

### X.B Binary finite-shot scaling and residual-correlation robustness

Suggested text:

> We next test the finite-shot and model-mismatch structure of the binary theorem directly. For balanced binary qubit records with Bloch contrasts `c=0.50,0.75,1.00`, same-event randomized Pauli-shadow snapshots are used to estimate the connected two-fragment operator. Across five sample sizes, log-log fits of the median decoder sin-angle error versus the number `N` of paired estimates give slopes `-0.489`, `-0.519`, and `-0.489`, respectively, close to the expected `N^{-1/2}` statistical scaling. The rescaled quantity `c^2 sqrt(N) sin(theta)` remains approximately constant (`3.6-4.0` across the tested contrasts), consistent with inverse conditioning by the rank-one signal scale `lambda proportional to c^2`. The explicit concentration bound is substantially more conservative than the observed error and is not presented as a tight performance prediction.
>
> To probe departure from conditional independence, we add a traceless two-fragment correlation term that preserves both local conditional marginals. Writing `nu=I(E_i:E_j|X)` in bits, the population decoder-direction error is fit over the tested physical range by `sin(theta)=1.662 sqrt(nu)+0.014` with `R^2=0.995`; finite-shot medians at `2N=16,384` track the same degradation. This supports the square-root perturbative dependence entering the Pinsker/Wedin robustness analysis, while again showing that the worst-case bound is conservative.

### X.C Explicit finite-shot virtual collision experiment

Suggested text:

> We finally test the decoder estimator in an end-to-end virtual experiment rather than inserting conditional record states directly. Starting from `|+>_S|0...0>_E`, five sequential controlled-`R_y` collisions generate a six-qubit system-environment state. Each environmental qubit is then conjugated by an independently unknown local `SU(2)` rotation. The learner has no system access and receives only finite-shot environmental Pauli outcomes from a 27-setting orthogonal-array schedule, with independent symmetric readout flips of probability `q=0.02`. Recovery is declared successful only when every one of the five learned local decoder axes lies within `5 deg` of its oracle Helstrom direction. At perfect local distinguishability `D=1`, strict success increases from `0.314` at 64 shots per setting to `0.800`, `0.971`, and `1.000` at 128, 256, and 512 shots. At `D=0.707`, the corresponding rates are `0`, `0.143`, `0.486`, and `0.829`. The phase boundary makes explicit the joint role of record strength and finite measurement budget. This benchmark is a hardware-informed simulation, not a device-calibrated experiment.

## Claims that are now well supported

- The equal-prior binary connected-operator estimator exhibits empirical `N^{-1/2}` finite-shot scaling with the expected inverse contrast conditioning in the tested qubit model.
- Controlled residual conditional correlations produce decoder-direction degradation approximately proportional to `sqrt(I(E_i:E_j|X))` in the tested perturbative family.
- The two-view multiclass ambiguity is exact at population level, whereas a representative well-conditioned third view permits increasingly reliable finite-sample latent/decoder recovery.
- Explicit microscopic collision dynamics with unknown fragmentwise local bases, finite Pauli statistics, and readout noise exhibit a clear record-strength/resource recovery boundary.

## Claims to avoid

Do not state that:

- the finite-shot theorem constants are tight;
- a third view is computationally or statistically efficient in every multiclass model;
- the conditional-correlation test covers arbitrary non-Markovian or correlated environments;
- the virtual collision benchmark is a hardware experiment;
- EIQL has a universal sample-complexity or measurement-setting advantage over tomography;
- all fragments are within 5 degrees when quoting the older mean-error success metric. Use the new strict all-fragment metric for that statement.
