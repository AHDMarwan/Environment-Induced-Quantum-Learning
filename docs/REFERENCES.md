# Literature map

This is a working map, not an exhaustive bibliography. The manuscript contains the formal bibliography.

## Foundations: decoherence, Quantum Darwinism, objective records

- W. H. Zurek, *Decoherence, einselection, and the quantum origins of the classical*, Rev. Mod. Phys. 75, 715 (2003).
- H. Ollivier, D. Poulin, W. H. Zurek, *Objective Properties from Subjective Quantum States: Environment as a Witness*, Phys. Rev. Lett. 93, 220401 (2004).
- H. Ollivier, D. Poulin, W. H. Zurek, *Environment as a witness: Selective proliferation of information and emergence of objectivity in a quantum universe*, Phys. Rev. A 72, 042113 (2005).
- W. H. Zurek, *Quantum Darwinism*, Nature Physics 5, 181–188 (2009).
- R. Horodecki, J. K. Korbicz, P. Horodecki, work on spectrum broadcast structure / quantum origins of objectivity.
- M. Le, A. Olaya-Castro, work on Strong Quantum Darwinism / strong independence / SBS.
- F. Fu, work on uniqueness of the observable leaving redundant imprints (2021).
- A. Touil et al., work on observer consensus and objective records.

## Neighboring learning problems

These areas must be distinguished explicitly from EIQL:

- learning an externally specified unknown quantum measurement;
- quantum self-supervised learning;
- unsupervised discovery of interpretable observables/order parameters from quantum data;
- classical multiview/common-information learning;
- adaptive quantum sensing / measurement design.

Current EIQL distinction: **the target decoder is not supplied; redundancy across environment fragments generated by the same physical event supplies the training signal.**
